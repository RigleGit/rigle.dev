CapCut for desktop: how to edit videos from scratch

Rodrigo Iglesias // 2024-05-06 // 460 words // 3 min

A CapCut tutorial for Windows and Mac: learn to cut clips, adjust audio, add automatic captions and export your video. Includes a step-by-step video tutorial.

Watch the full tutorial on YouTube (in Spanish).

CapCut started as a mobile app, but it also has a desktop version for Windows and Mac. If you want to put your clips together, add music and create captions, you can do it all there. I recorded this tutorial to explain the process from scratch, without assuming you already know how to edit.

You can download the app from CapCut’s official website. Here are the main steps from the video for easy reference.

Getting started with CapCut

A new project has plenty of buttons, but start with four areas: import files and find tools on the left; preview the result in the centre; adjust the selected item on the right; and build your video on the timeline at the bottom.

Drag clips or photos onto that timeline. You can rearrange them and trim their edges. To cut a clip in two, place the playhead at the cut and use Split (Ctrl + B on Windows). I show this from 3:38 in the video.

Music, layers and transitions

Audio goes on its own track. You can trim it just like a video and add fades at the beginning and end so it does not start or stop abruptly. If you are speaking, turn the music down enough to keep your voice clear.

Images and videos can overlap too. A higher track covers whatever is below it; adjust its opacity to blend the two. To add a transition, drag it between two clips and change its duration. Try a few, but you do not need a different one at every cut.

How to add automatic captions

This is one of the features you can get plenty of use from. In the version shown in the tutorial, go to Text → Auto captions:

  1. Select the audio language and generate the captions.
  2. Check the text and split long sentences into shorter blocks.
  3. Adjust the font, size and outline, then use Apply to all to keep the style consistent.

In the video, I try splitting them every two or three words. That can make them feel quicker, but they still need to be easy to read.

Exporting your video

Click Export and choose MP4 with H.264. If you worked in 1080p, export at that resolution. For a talking-head video, 30 fps is usually enough; if you recorded gameplay at 60 fps, you can keep that frame rate. In the tutorial, I use Recommended or Higher for the bitrate.

Before calling it finished, play the exported file. It is a good way to catch captions that disappear too quickly or music that is louder than you realised.
